# X2. The zero state — what a man entering the modern world with nothing actually has, lacks, and faces

## 0. What this file is and how to read it

The book is being written from thirteen military texts for a man who has just entered the modern world and has nothing. Before any doctrine is applied to him, his position has to be described the way a commander describes his own force and situation: inventory, terrain, constraints, sequence. Not encouragement. Not a picture of how bad it is. An inventory.

**Scope and defaults.** A single adult, US, 2026, metropolitan, physically able, legally present, with no capital, income, shelter, network, credential or reserve. Where other rich countries differ materially, that is said at the point of difference. Age, race, sex, disability, immigration status and criminal record all change the numbers substantially and are handled where the evidence exists rather than flattened into an average case.

**Evidence rule.** Every number below carries the source I opened and the year of the data, which is often not the year of the report. Where a figure is commonly cited and I could not open a source for it, I have not stated the figure; section 8 lists those refusals by name. Where the claim is my inference rather than a source's finding, it is marked *my judgment*. Two of the best-known citations in this literature needed correction on contact — the CFPB's credit-invisible estimate and the cognitive-scarcity result — and both corrections are in the text rather than hidden.

**A caution about the genre.** Most writing about starting with nothing exists to raise money or to raise morale. Both distort the same way: they overstate the leverage of individual decisions and understate the mechanical gates. The gates below are mechanical. They do not respond to attitude.

## 1. The inventory of what he has

### 1.1 A body and its hours

The only unencumbered asset. Roughly 16 waking hours a day, of which perhaps 10 to 12 are usefully deployable before performance degrades, and that number falls fast without secure sleep — which belongs in section 3, because sleep is a constraint, not a choice.

What the hours are worth is set by the floor of the labour market, not by effort. The federal minimum wage is **$7.25 per hour, unchanged since 24 July 2009** ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage)). Most states and many cities set higher minimums, but $7.25 is the legal floor and the number a man with no leverage can be offered in the states that have not raised it.

The realistic range: in the second quarter of 2026, median usual weekly earnings for the 120.9 million US full-time wage and salary workers were **$1,251**; the first decile was **$642 a week** and the first quartile **$850** ([BLS, Q2 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/wkyeng.nr0.htm); [Table 5](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/wkyeng.t05.htm)). A man entering at the bottom is looking at the $642 line, full-time, before tax — roughly $33,000 a year if he holds the job a full year, which at entry level he probably will not.

*My judgment:* the body's value at zero is not its wage rate but that it is the one asset nobody can foreclose, garnish or repossess, and the only one deployable the same day.

### 1.2 Legal personhood

He can enter contracts, own property, sue and be sued, work, move between states without permission, and refuse. Genuinely load-bearing — most of human history and much of the present world does not grant it — and close to worthless in isolation. The right to enter a lease is not an apartment. Personhood converts to advantage only when combined with documents, which is section 4's problem.

The one place it has direct cash value: **emergency medical care**. Under EMTALA (1986), Medicare-participating hospitals offering emergency services must provide a medical screening examination and stabilising treatment for an emergency medical condition "regardless of an individual's ability to pay" ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/legislation/emergency-medical-treatment-labor-act)). The limits matter: the obligation is to screen and stabilise, not to treat. Nothing chronic, nothing dental, no medication after discharge, and it generates a bill that becomes a collections account. A floor against dying in the street, not health care. Every other rich country's equivalent is a general entitlement to treatment rather than a stabilisation duty, and that is the largest structural difference between the US zero state and the Canadian, British, German, French, Japanese or Australian ones.

### 1.3 Public goods

**Libraries.** Approximately 9,000 public library systems comprising over 17,000 individual outlets, with public-access internet computers up 55 percent over the decade to the mid-2010s ([IMLS, September 2017, on FY2014–15 data](https://www.imls.gov/news/imls-releases-annual-data-and-survey-results-american-public-libraries)). The most underrated asset in the inventory: free heated shelter during business hours, a chair, a power outlet, internet, a printer, staff who answer questions, and in many systems free database and course access and a mailing address of last resort.

**Free instruction.** The technical content of most paid entry-level training is available without charge. What is not free is the credential, the structure and the accountability. *My judgment:* this asymmetry is the defining feature of the modern zero state, and section 5 turns on it — information is free, sorting is not.

**Public transit.** Access to a metropolitan labour market without a car. Enormous where it exists, near zero where it does not; this one variable determines whether a man without capital can reach the jobs at all.

**Public records.** Court, corporate, property and regulatory filings are open, and almost nobody at the bottom uses them. *My judgment:* a real and cheap edge, unused because learning what the records mean takes time — exactly the resource section 3 says he does not have.

### 1.4 The open door of the labour market

Employers at the bottom of the market hire without credentials, without references and frequently without a real interview, because turnover is high and screening is expensive relative to the wage. It is a genuine door, and the only large one open on day one.

Its limits are severe. These jobs pay near the first decile; they are scheduled unpredictably, which forecloses the second job and the evening class; they build no transferable credential; and they are sorted by the background-check machinery in section 2. The BLS counted **4.4 million people employed part time for economic reasons** in August 2026 — people who wanted full-time work and could not get it ([BLS, August 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)). The open door does not guarantee full-time hours behind it.

### 1.5 The option value of anonymity

No reputation means no bad reputation: no public record of failure, no stale salary history anchoring his price, no incumbent identity to defend, no institutional enemies. He can change city, industry and story at near-zero cost.

*My judgment:* real, but chronically overstated, because anonymity is an option and options expire unexercised. Its value lies entirely in the cheap reversible bet a man with a mortgage and a title cannot make. Unexercised, it is simply obscurity, which is the default state of everyone who fails.

### 1.6 Time horizon

If he is young, decades: the only asset that compounds without maintenance, and the only one strictly non-renewable. Section 5 treats it properly.

## 2. The inventory of what he lacks, and the mechanism of each lack

The discipline here is to name the *mechanism*, not the deficiency. "He lacks a network" is a sentiment. "Referred applicants are more likely to be hired even though referrals and nonreferrals have similar skill characteristics" is a mechanism, and it says what is actually happening at the gate.

### 2.1 Capital and reserve

Not merely no savings — no buffer against variance. The Federal Reserve's 2025 survey found **63 percent of US adults could cover a $400 emergency expense using cash or its equivalent**, unchanged from 2024 ([Federal Reserve, issued 13 May 2026, fielded October 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20260513a.htm)). He sits below the 37 percent who could not, having nothing at all.

*Mechanism:* without a reserve, every unexpected cost is met by borrowing at the rates available to someone with no credit record (§6.1), by liquidating a productive asset — tools, transport — or by default. Each response raises the cost of the next shock.

### 2.2 Credit history and credit score

Not bad credit — no credit. The CFPB calls this being "credit invisible," and its widely cited 2015 estimate was corrected in June 2025: after data corrections, an estimated **5.8 percent of adults (13.5 million) were credit invisible in December 2010**, not the published 11.0 percent (25.9 million), while the share with an *unscored* record rose from 7.4 to 12.7 percent (17.2 to 29.7 million). By December 2020 the credit-invisible share was **2.7 percent (7.0 million)**, and the share with a scored record had risen from 81.6 to 87.5 percent ([CFPB, June 2025](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_update-credit-invisibles-estimate_2025-06.pdf)).

So: the population with no credit record at all is smaller than the famous number, and the population whose record exists but cannot be scored is larger. He is in one group or the other, and either forecloses the same doors.

*Mechanism:* FICO's published weighting is payment history 35 percent, amounts owed 30 percent, length of credit history 15 percent, new credit 10 percent, credit mix 10 percent ([myFICO](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score)). Note what that does to a man at zero: half the model — payment history plus length — is a function of elapsed time under observation, which no amount of income or diligence compresses. This is the clearest gate in the inventory that only time opens, and the practical consequence is to start the clock early, before credit is needed, because its value accrues with age rather than with use.

### 2.3 Shelter, and the screening that gates it

*Mechanism, stated carefully because this is the gate that fails most often.* Landlords do not evaluate applicants; they buy a report and an algorithmic score. The CFPB found that prior rental payment history — the single most predictive fact about a tenant — is overwhelmingly absent from those reports: industry estimates of coverage range between **1.7 percent and 2.3 percent of US renters**. Corporate landlords' automated systems "substitute a single algorithmic score for the more nuanced and holistic evaluation of prospective tenants done historically by smaller landlords." **68 percent of renters pay application fees**, funding reports they usually never see; of more than 24,000 complaints analysed, over 16,000 concerned incorrect information and 4,500 concerned obstacles to fixing errors ([CFPB, 15 November 2022](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-reports-highlight-problems-with-tenant-background-checks/)).

Operationally: the fee is charged per application and not refunded on rejection, so the search itself is metered by cash; the score is built on criminal and eviction records plus credit, none of which he can supply favourably; and the one fact that would help him — that he pays rent on time — is structurally invisible. *My judgment:* this is why the smaller, non-corporate landlord is not a preference at the bottom but a structural necessity. It is the only route where a human being can be persuaded.

### 2.4 Network

*Mechanism:* referrals are not a courtesy but a hiring technology, used because they work. Burks, Cowgill, Hoffman and Housman, using personnel data from nine large firms across call centres, trucking and high-tech, found that "compared to nonreferred applicants, referred applicants are more likely to be hired and more likely to accept offers, even though referrals and nonreferrals have similar skill characteristics," and that referred workers are "substantially less likely to quit," producing higher profit per worker through lower turnover and recruiting costs ([*Quarterly Journal of Economics* 130(2), 2015, 805–839](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v130y2015i2p805-839.html)).

Read that precisely. *Similar skill characteristics.* The referral is not selecting better workers; it is buying the firm a cheaper, lower-variance hire. The unreferred applicant is not losing a contest on merit — he is absent from the cheaper channel, and the channel is where the decision happens.

The population evidence agrees. Chetty and colleagues, using privacy-protected data on **21 billion Facebook friendships**, found that if children with low-income parents grew up in counties with economic connectedness comparable to that of the average child with high-income parents, "their incomes in adulthood would increase by 20% on average" — equivalent to growing up in a family making $47,000 instead of $27,000. Other forms of social capital, including how tight-knit a community is and levels of civic engagement, were **not** strongly associated with mobility ([Opportunity Insights summary, August 2022, of two papers in *Nature*](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/socialcapital_nontech.pdf)).

That last clause is the useful one. What predicts mobility is not belonging to a community but holding relationships across the class line. A dense network of people in his own position was measured and found not to help.

### 2.5 Credential and the hiring filter

He lacks the artefact that lets a stranger stop evaluating him. Public two-year in-district tuition and fees averaged **$4,150** in 2025–26, from $1,440 in California to $8,900 in Vermont; public four-year in-state averaged $11,950 ([College Board, Trends in College Pricing 2025](https://research.collegeboard.org/trends/college-pricing/highlights)). Community college is cheap absolutely and unaffordable in cash-flow terms to a man with no reserve; the binding constraint is usually the schedule, not the tuition.

*Mechanism:* the credential's function is not to prove competence but to survive a filter cheaply. Screening is a cost the employer minimises; the credential reduces it. Any substitute must be equally cheap to check — verifiable work in public, a named referee, a test the employer already runs.

### 2.6 Reputation and the record that substitutes for it

He has no evidence of reliability. What stands in its place, if he has one, is a criminal record, and the audit evidence is unambiguous.

Devah Pager's Milwaukee audit sent matched pairs of testers with identical credentials to 350 entry-level openings. **34 percent of whites without criminal records received callbacks, against 17 percent of whites with a criminal record** — the record halved the rate. Race ran deeper: **14 percent of blacks without criminal records received callbacks**, below the rate for whites *with* a record. The non-offender to ex-offender ratio was 2:1 for whites, nearly 3:1 for blacks. Roughly 75 percent of employers asked about criminal history on the application form ([Devah Pager, *The Mark of a Criminal Record*, working-paper version, Northwestern IPR, 2002; published *American Journal of Sociology* 108(5), 2003](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2002/IPR-WP-02-37.pdf)).

Downstream: among approximately 5 million formerly incarcerated people, unemployment ran **over 27 percent** ([Prison Policy Initiative, Couloute and Kopf, July 2018](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/outofwork.html)).

*Age of evidence, stated rather than buried:* Pager's fieldwork is from 2001, and ban-the-box laws have since moved the question later in many jurisdictions. The mechanism — a cheap negative signal terminating review before anything else is read — has not changed, and the 2018 figure is consistent with it.

### 2.7 Tools, and the information about how things work

Tools are a capital problem and largely a solved one; most cognitive work now needs a used laptop and connectivity. The deeper lack is procedural knowledge: what the steps are, in what order, what a thing costs, what is negotiable, what a normal salary is, which institutions have discretion, what a deadline means.

*Mechanism:* procedural knowledge is not published, because it is worth nothing to the people who hold it and transmits inside families and workplaces as a by-product of proximity. From the inside its absence does not look like ignorance; it looks like the world being arbitrary. *My judgment:* the most expensive lack and the least visible, because a man who does not know a step exists cannot search for it.

### 2.8 Documents, health, status

Documents are section 4, being the first link in the chain. Untreated chronic illness or addiction converts every other item in this inventory into a maintenance problem. Absent or irregular immigration status changes the analysis so fundamentally — no lawful work, no ID, no bank account, no complaint to any authority without risk — that it is a different book, and this file does not pretend to cover it.

## 3. The constraints that do not negotiate

### 3.1 Rent

The constraint that sets every other number. In August 2026 the national median rent was **$1,390 a month across all unit sizes, $1,221 for a one-bedroom**, with the national multifamily vacancy rate at 7.1 percent ([Apartment List, August 2026](https://www.apartmentlist.com/research/national-rent-data)).

Against the wage: the National Low Income Housing Coalition's *Out of Reach 2026* put the national **two-bedroom Housing Wage at $34.73 an hour and the one-bedroom at $29.19** — what a full-time worker must earn to rent modestly at 30 percent of income. The average hourly wage earned by renters was **$24.84**. In 49 states, DC and Puerto Rico, renters earning the average renter wage must work more than 40 hours a week to afford a modest two-bedroom ([NLIHC, 23 July 2026](https://nlihc.org/news/nlihc-releases-out-reach-2026-high-cost-housing)). Against the federal minimum of $7.25, the one-bedroom Housing Wage is slightly over four times the wage.

Downstream: renter cost burdens hit a record in 2024, with **22.7 million households spending more than 30 percent of income on rent and utilities and 12.1 million spending more than half**, an overall burden rate of 49 percent. Among renters earning under $30,000, **83 percent were cost burdened and 67 percent severely so**, and the median renter in that group had **$210 left each month after rent and utilities — down 60 percent since 2001**. Sixty percent of renter households headed by an adult under 25 were burdened ([Joint Center for Housing Studies, *America's Rental Housing 2026*](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/americas-rental-housing-2026)).

That $210 is the most important number in this file: the entire monthly budget, for the median household in his income band, for food, transport, phone, medicine, tools, training and emergencies, after housing. Not a story about poor choices. What is left.

The failure state is measurable: **745,652 people experiencing homelessness on a single night in January 2025, of whom 266,320 were unsheltered** ([HUD, 29 May 2026](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-037)).

*Other rich countries:* housing cost pressure is not a US peculiarity — it is severe in Canada, Australia, Ireland and urban Britain — but social housing stock, security-of-tenure law and housing allowances materially change how a zero-state individual meets it. I have not sourced comparative figures and state none.

### 3.2 Food

Cheap relative to rent, non-negotiable in the short run. The federal floor is legible in SNAP: for FY2026, effective **1 October 2025 through 30 September 2026**, the maximum monthly allotment in the 48 states and DC is **$298 for a one-person household and $546 for two**, with a standard deduction of $209 for households of one to three, a maximum excess shelter deduction of $744 and a homeless shelter deduction of $198.99 ([USDA FNS](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/resource-files/snap-fy26maximumAllotments-deductions.pdf)).

$298 a month is roughly $9.80 a day, and it is the government's own estimate of a minimally adequate food budget under the Thrifty Food Plan. Set it against the $210 of residual income above and the arithmetic of the bottom is clear: without SNAP, food alone exceeds what is left after rent.

### 3.3 Sleep

Not a preference. Cognitive performance, emotional regulation and judgment degrade with sleep deprivation, and the degradation is not subjectively visible to the person experiencing it. *My judgment, not a sourced claim:* insecure sleep is the mechanism by which housing insecurity converts directly into employment failure, because it attacks reliability — the only quality the bottom of the labour market is actually buying. A man sleeping in a car or a shelter with a 6 a.m. clear-out is not choosing between housing and work. He is losing the work because of the housing.

### 3.4 The law

It does not bend, it applies unevenly, and enforcement against someone without a lawyer is effectively automatic. An unpaid fine becomes a warrant; a warrant becomes an arrest; an arrest becomes a record; the record forecloses housing and employment per §2.6. Each step is cheap for the system and catastrophic for him.

### 3.5 The cost of time

Every bureaucratic step costs hours in a queue during business hours — the hours he would otherwise be paid for. A man with an unpredictable hourly schedule pays for every appointment twice: the lost shift and the risk to the job. This converts free services into expensive ones and is the main reason free services go unused.

### 3.6 Bureaucratic gates and their dependency chain

The structural trap of the whole zero state, documented in the regulations themselves.

To obtain a REAL ID — the standard state identification — an applicant must at minimum document: "1) Full Legal Name; 2) Date of Birth; 3) Social Security Number; 4) **Two Proofs of Address of Principal Residence**; and 5) Lawful Status." Enforcement began 7 May 2025 ([DHS](https://www.dhs.gov/real-id-frequently-asked-questions)). Two proofs of address — from a man who has no address.

To open a bank account, 31 CFR 1020.220 requires the bank to obtain name, date of birth, address and an identification number, verified documentarily by "unexpired government-issued identification evidencing nationality or residence and bearing a photograph" ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)). So: ID requires address; bank requires ID; employer requires bank, or charges for the alternative; address requires income; income requires the job.

The regulation contains the escape, and almost nobody knows it. For an individual without a residential or business street address, the required address "may include an Army Post Office (APO) or Fleet Post Office (FPO) box number, **or the residential or business street address of next of kin or of another contact individual**." That clause is the legal hinge of section 4. One person willing to let him use an address converts an impossible chain into a possible one — a precise, mechanical statement of why the network deficit in §2.4 is a binding constraint rather than a soft one.

One more forgiving link: **Social Security card replacements are free**, and if the card is unavailable the SSN can be documented for REAL ID purposes by a W-2, an SSA-1099, a non-SSA-1099, or a pay stub bearing the applicant's name and SSN ([USA.gov, updated 16 January 2026](https://www.usa.gov/social-security-card); DHS, above).

### 3.7 The compounding cost of emergencies without reserves

A man with a reserve meets a $600 car repair with $600. A man without one meets it with a payday loan at the rates in §6.1, or by losing the car, and therefore the job, and therefore the apartment. The same event does not cost the two men the same, and the difference is not linear. *My judgment:* this asymmetry — identical shocks priced by reserve position — is the most important structural fact in the inventory, and the one the strategy has to be built around.

## 4. The real sequence

The dependency graph, in the order the gates open. *The ordering is my judgment*, derived from the constraints above; the specifics of each step are sourced.

**Step 0 — Documents.** Social Security card first: replacement is free (§3.6). Then birth certificate from the state of birth, the root document, which carries a fee in most states. Then state ID, which needs the two proofs of address — so the address problem is solved before the ID, not after.

**Step 1 — An address.** Not shelter, a *legal address*. The regulatory route is the next-of-kin or contact-individual address permitted under 31 CFR 1020.220 (§3.6). The practical routes are general delivery, a shelter or transitional programme that provides an address, or one willing person. This is where the network deficit becomes binding and where most zero-state trajectories fail.

**Step 2 — A phone number.** A stable number is the precondition for every callback in §2.6. The federal Lifeline programme subsidises phone or broadband for qualifying low-income households, administered by the Universal Service Administrative Company. *I did not open a current Lifeline benefit page and state no figure.*

**Step 3 — A bank account.** With ID and address, the CIP requirements in §3.6 are satisfiable. Do it before income, not after: **4.2 percent of US households (about 5.6 million) were unbanked in 2023**, the most-cited main reason "don't have enough money to meet minimum balance requirements" and the second "don't trust banks." Unbanked households disproportionately use nonbank money orders, check cashing and money transfer for core transactions — 8.0 percent of all households used nonbank money orders, 2.7 percent nonbank check cashing ([FDIC, 2023 survey, released November 2024](https://www.fdic.gov/household-survey)). Every one of those transactions carries a fee a bank account does not.

**Step 4 — Income.** The open door of §1.4. Take the job that starts soonest, not the best one; its function is to make steps 5 and 6 possible, not to be a career.

**Step 5 — Stabilised shelter.** Against the screening in §2.3: expect a fee per attempt, expect the score to be unfavourable, target the landlord with discretion. A room in a shared unit is not a compromise here. It is the only option that leaves residual income above the $210 line in §3.1.

**Step 6 — A reserve.** Before skill, before credential. Per §3.7: without one, the first shock undoes steps 1 through 5 and returns him to step 0 having spent a year. A reserve is not savings. It is insurance against repeating the sequence.

**Step 7 — Skill, then position.** In that order: skill without a position is unpaid, a position without skill does not survive contact. Start the credit clock from §2.2 somewhere around step 3, because it is the one gate that only elapsed time opens.

*The step most often skipped is 6, and skipping it is why the sequence is usually run more than once.*

## 5. What compounds and what is consumed

The distinction the book turns on. An asset compounds if holding it increases the rate at which more arrives; it is consumed if spending it reduces the stock.

**Compounds.** *Skill*, because competence lowers the cost of acquiring adjacent competence. *Reputation*, because evidence of reliability is cheap for strangers to check and each use adds to it. *Relationships*, specifically cross-class ones — the Chetty finding in §2.4 measures exactly this and finds same-class density does not substitute. *Savings*, mechanically, and because a reserve converts shocks from catastrophes into expenses. *Health*, better described as a stock that compounds negatively when neglected. *Credit history*, uniquely, because it compounds on elapsed time rather than effort (§2.2).

**Consumed.** *Time*, absolutely and irreversibly. *Attention*, daily and non-storable. *Goodwill* — a favour asked is a favour spent, and the stock replenishes only through reciprocity, which requires something to give. *Novelty*, the option value of anonymity in §1.5, which decays as the record accumulates whether or not he used it.

**The load-bearing observation.** Everything that compounds requires a surplus to invest, and the constraints in section 3 leave no surplus — not by intent, but in effect. The $210 residual income figure is the compounding problem stated as a number. This is what the development literature calls a poverty trap, and it is the honest answer to why effort alone does not resolve the zero state: effort produces income, income is absorbed by non-negotiating constraints, and nothing reaches the compounding assets.

Two flags. Banerjee and Duflo, who with Michael Kremer received the 2019 Sveriges Riksbank Prize "for their experimental approach to alleviating global poverty" ([Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2019/summary/)), argue in *Poor Economics* (2011) that the poor face the same decisions as everyone else with far less margin for error and far worse institutional infrastructure — that what looks like bad judgment is usually the absence of the defaults, insurance and automatic systems that protect better-off people from their own identical judgment. *That is the book's argument, which I have read, not a statistic from a page opened for this file; no number is attributed to it.*

Mullainathan and Shafir's *Scarcity* (2013) argues that scarcity itself consumes cognitive bandwidth, and the underlying paper (Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir and Zhao, "Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function," *Science* 341, 2013) is among the most cited in the field. **It is contested.** A preregistered replication audit reported a low replication rate for the scarcity literature, and the original authors published a PNAS rebuttal titled "A scarcity literature mischaracterized with an empirical audit." **I could not open either paper and state no figures, and no publication years, from either side.** The honest position: treat bandwidth depletion as a plausible mechanism with disputed support, and build no doctrine on it. The mechanical constraints in section 3 produce the same outcome with no psychology at all.

## 6. The known traps

Each of these is a mechanism that converts a man's effort into someone else's revenue while leaving his position unchanged or worse.

### 6.1 Predatory credit

The best-documented trap in the inventory. CFPB research found the median fee on a storefront payday loan is **$15 per $100 borrowed** with a median term of 14 days, an **APR of 391 percent** on a median loan of $350. More than **80 percent of payday loans were rolled over or reborrowed within 30 days**; borrowers **default 20 percent of the time**. Half of online payday borrowers had at least one failed debit or overdraft over 18 months, incurring an average of **$185 in bank penalty fees**, and **36 percent of accounts with a failed debit from an online lender were closed by the bank**, usually within 90 days of the first non-sufficient-funds transaction. In 2015 there were 15,766 payday stores across 36 states, against 14,350 McDonald's outlets nationwide in 2014 ([CFPB, 2 June 2016](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/Payday_Loans_Highlights_From_CFPB_Research.pdf)).

Read the 36 percent against section 4. A payday loan does not merely cost 391 percent. In roughly a third of failed cases it destroys step 3 of the sequence — the bank account — and returns him to the fee-paying nonbank economy.

### 6.2 MLMs and pay-to-work schemes

FTC staff analysed **70 publicly available MLM income disclosure statements** and found that "most participants made $1,000 or less per year — that's less than $84 dollars per month," and that "in at least 17 MLMs, most participants didn't make any money at all." The statements routinely emphasise top performers, omit data on participants earning nothing, and ignore participant expenses that often exceed income ([FTC, 4 September 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/09/ftc-staff-report-analyzes-70-mlm-income-disclosure-statements)).

*General form:* any arrangement where he pays to work — paid "training" for a job not yet offered, mandatory equipment purchase, starter kits, bottom-of-market franchise fees. The test is directional. Money flowing from him toward the opportunity means the opportunity's revenue is him.

### 6.3 False credentials

Certifications that cost money and are not checked. The test is §2.5's: does anyone on the hiring side verify it, and does verifying reduce their screening cost? If not, it is a product sold to applicants, not a signal bought by employers.

### 6.4 The gig-work treadmill

*My judgment, with the evidence I have rather than the evidence I would want.* Gig work converts hours into cash with near-zero onboarding, which is genuinely valuable at step 4. Its trap is structural: it builds no credential, no referee, no referral relationship and no seniority, so a year of it leaves the §5 compounding assets where they started while consuming the one asset that does not replenish. It also loads vehicle depreciation, maintenance and insurance onto the worker, invisibly to the headline hourly figure. *I could not open a current credible source on platform net earnings after vehicle costs and state no earnings figure.*

### 6.5 Addiction

Converts every asset in section 1 into financing for the addiction and every constraint in section 3 into a crisis. Nothing else here survives contact with it.

### 6.6 The cost of appearing poor

Pratchett's "boots theory" — the poor man buys cheap boots repeatedly and outspends the rich man who buys one good pair — is a literary formulation from *Men at Arms* (1993), not a research finding, and should be cited as such.

The phenomenon is real and parts of it are sourced above: rental application fees paid per attempt with no refund on rejection, in a system where the score is unfavourable and the favourable data absent (§2.3); nonbank check cashing and money order fees for transactions a bank account performs free (§4); credit at 391 percent instead of a card rate, and bank penalties averaging $185 over 18 months (§6.1). In each case the same good costs more because of the buyer's position rather than the good's quality.

**I could not open a source for any aggregate "poverty premium" dollar figure and state none.** The circulated totals come from UK research I could not open. The mechanism is documented; the aggregate is not.

### 6.7 How people who start with nothing stay there

Structural, not moral, and the four mechanisms are all above. *Residual income near zero* (§3.1) means no surplus reaches any compounding asset. *Shocks priced by reserve position* (§3.7) means each event costs him more than it costs someone with savings. *Gates that only time opens* (§2.2, §2.6) means some doors cannot be forced by effort at all. And *the sorting mechanisms are cheap negative filters* (§2.3, §2.6), built to exclude quickly rather than to identify merit — so the correct response is to reach the humans with discretion, not to improve the application.

The macro picture is consistent. Absolute income mobility — the fraction of children earning more than their parents — **fell from approximately 90 percent for children born in 1940 to 50 percent for those born in the 1980s**, and from 95 to 41 percent comparing sons' earnings to fathers' for the 1984 cohort ([Chetty, Hendren, Grusky, Hell, Manduca and Narang, *Science* 356(6336): 398–406, 2017](https://opportunityinsights.org/paper/the-fading-american-dream/)). Geography matters enormously: the probability a child from the bottom income quintile reaches the top quintile was **4.4 percent in Charlotte and 12.9 percent in San Jose** ([Chetty, Hendren, Kline and Saez, *Where is the Land of Opportunity?*, *QJE*, 2014](https://opportunityinsights.org/paper/land-of-opportunity/)).

*That three-to-one geographic spread is the most actionable statistic in this file*, and the one that appears least often in advice literature, because the implied instruction — move — is unwelcome.

Failure is ordinary rather than dramatic. The Eviction Lab records **2,350,042 eviction filings and 898,479 evictions in 2016** — a 6.12 percent filing rate, 2.34 percent eviction rate; across 2000–2016, "roughly 1 eviction filing for every 17 renter households," with about 1 in 40 renter households evicted ([Eviction Lab, 2018, with a 2023 note directing users to revised data](https://evictionlab.org/national-estimates/)). In 2025, landlords filed over **1.23 million eviction cases** across the 10 states and 38 cities the Lab tracks — home to roughly a third of US renter households — at an average filing rate of 7.9 percent, against 1.25 million in 2024 ([Eviction Lab, April 2026](https://evictionlab.org/ets-report-2025/)).

Matthew Desmond's *Evicted* (2016) is the ethnography behind the Lab and makes the argument these numbers support: eviction is not merely a consequence of poverty but a cause of it, because it destroys address, job, possessions and rental record at once, and the eviction record then feeds back into the screening system in §2.3. *That is the book's argument as I understand it; no statistic is attributed to it, and the numbers above are the Lab's own.*

## 7. What the canon's soldiers had that he does not

The book is built from thirteen military texts, and the translation problem must be stated before any is applied, because the soldier in those texts is not a man alone. He is the most heavily supported individual in his society.

**A unit.** Sun Tzu, Vegetius, Maurice and de Saxe all assume a formation; the individual soldier's survival is a function of the men beside him, and he is never the whole force. The man here is a force of one, and every doctrine of concentration, reserve, relief and rotation assumes a plurality he does not have.

**A commander.** Someone whose job is to decide where the force goes. He is simultaneously sovereign, general and private — and the Sun Tzu file notes that the separation of sovereign from general is the structural premise of that entire book. He cannot delegate, cannot receive orders, and has nobody to disobey.

**Rations and logistics.** Vegetius attends seriously to supply because the army is fed by an apparatus; the soldier does not source his own food while fighting. Section 3.2 is this man's commissariat, and it comes out of the same hours as his campaign.

**A doctrine.** Transmitted procedure for the ordinary case — how to march, camp, dig, form up. He has none, and §2.7 says that absence is his most expensive lack. This is what the book is attempting to supply, and why it cannot be supplied by translating maxims.

**A state.** The largest gap. Behind the soldier stands a polity that recruits, trains, pays, arms, feeds, ransoms and buries him, and supplies the purpose the campaign serves. He has a state that guarantees his personhood, will stabilise him in an emergency room, and will otherwise not notice him. Nobody's interest requires that he succeed.

**A defined enemy, a defined objective, a season and an end.** War has a shape; his situation has none, and the absence of a terminating condition is a difference in kind, not degree.

### What he has that they did not

**He is not required to be there.** The conscript in Vegetius, the peasant levy in Sun Tzu, the line infantry in de Saxe — none could leave. He can quit, move, change field, refuse the engagement. Sun Tzu's doctrine of refusing battle on unfavourable ground is actually available to him; for the soldiers it was a commander's privilege.

**He cannot be killed by the loss, and no one else dies because he is wrong.** Failure is expensive and reversible. Almost every decision he faces is a bet he survives losing — a structurally different risk profile from every text in the canon, and one that changes which doctrines apply at all.

**He has intelligence they could not have bought.** Wage distributions, rent indices, mobility rates by county, the actual text of the regulations that gate him: free at a library terminal. Every commander in the canon would have paid heavily for equivalent knowledge of his own terrain.

**He has time in a quantity no campaign ever had.** The canon is written under a clock — the season, the harvest, the treasury, the enemy's movement — and Sun Tzu's whole doctrine of speed follows from an economy that could not afford a long war. This man can afford a long war. He cannot afford an expensive one.

*My judgment, and the practical conclusion of this file:* the canon's doctrines of concentration, sequencing, refusing unfavourable engagements, reconnaissance before commitment, and protecting the base translate. Its doctrines of command, morale management, supply discipline and decisive battle do not, because they presuppose a unit and a state. Any chapter that quietly imports the unit — that addresses him as though he had men, a commissariat and a sovereign behind him — has smuggled in the thing he most conspicuously lacks, and should be cut.
