# X3. The modern terrain

A map of the ground the book's man actually stands on: the United States, 2026, a developed economy, and a man who has just arrived in it with nothing. The canon in `corpus/01`–`corpus/13` describes ground, supply, fortification, intelligence, weather and enemies. This file establishes what those categories denote now, so a chapter saying "this is Sun Tzu's dispersive ground" or "this is Vauban's approach by parallels" can be held to account for the claim.

## 0. Method, and a warning about this genre

The failure mode of strategy-for-life writing is the free analogy: the author notices that a job interview has a winner and a loser, declares it a battle, and then imports the whole apparatus of war without asking whether the imported machinery has anything to grip. The Art of War becomes a decoration on ordinary careerism. This file exists to make that failure detectable. Three labels are used throughout, and they are not decorative:

***Fact.*** A claim about how a system works, or a number, traceable to a source I opened. Sources are listed in §10; where I could not open the primary document, the claim is marked and appears in the register at §11 rather than being laundered into the body as established.

***Analogy.*** An assertion that a modern structure and a military structure share a mechanism — not a mood, a mechanism. Every analogy is stated with its breaking point. An analogy without one is a lie of omission, because the reader will carry it past the point where it holds.

***Judgment.*** My own inference. It may be wrong, and is labelled so it can be rejected without taking the facts down with it.

One warning about the ground itself. The most contaminated area in this domain is job-search advice, where a folklore statistic — that 70 or 80 percent of jobs are never advertised, the "hidden job market" — circulates with no traceable modern basis. The origin appears to be a 1960s figure about newspaper advertising practice in a single city, repeated for sixty years past its context. I could not verify it against any current data and do not use it. That the most-repeated number in the field is unsourced folklore should set the reader's posture toward everything else he is told here, including by me.

## 1. Ground

In the canon, ground is the surface that decides whether force can be applied. Sun Tzu's nine grounds, Vegetius on the higher position, Vauban's soil determining whether a trench can be dug — all rest on one premise: position is prior to strength, and the map constrains every plan drawn on it. The modern equivalents are the labour market, the housing market, and physical location.

**How jobs are actually found and filled.** *Fact.* In July 2026 the US economy contained about 7.3 million job openings; employers made about 5.1 million hires that month against about 5.1 million separations, of which 3.1 million were voluntary quits and 1.7 million layoffs and discharges. In August 2026 there were about 7.0 million unemployed and an unemployment rate of 4.1 percent. Two structural facts follow, neither obvious. First, the aggregate ratio of openings to unemployed people is roughly one to one — there is no national shortage of positions, so failure to find work is generally not an arithmetic problem but a matching and gatekeeping problem. Second, churn is enormous relative to stock: five million hires a month against seven million unemployed means the market is not a queue to be waited out but a river to be entered. *Judgment.* The most important thing a man with nothing can know is that the labour market is not scarce in openings. It is scarce in the thing that converts an opening into a hire, which is a credible signal reaching a decision-maker.

*Fact.* On referrals, the honest position is narrower than the folklore and still substantial. Applicant-tracking data aggregated across more than 1,000 companies, covering over 14 million applications and 329,000 hires, found employee referrals produced more than 30 percent of all hires and 45 percent of internal hires. That figure is vendor-produced and from 2016; treat it as an order of magnitude, not a constant. The academic evidence is better founded on what a referral does. A study of one large firm's referral system found referred candidates more likely to be hired, enjoying an initial wage advantage that decays over time, and staying longer, with effects strongest at lower skill levels. A study of nine large firms across call centres, trucking and high technology found referred applicants more likely to be hired and to accept offers despite similar measured skill, and substantially less likely to quit.

*Mechanism.* The referral does not work by affection. It works because hiring is a decision under severe uncertainty about an unobservable — will this person show up, stay, and not be a disaster — and a referral is a costly, reputation-backed signal about exactly that unobservable, supplied by someone the firm already trusts. This is why the effect is strongest at lower skill levels: that is where formal credentials carry the least information and the uncertainty is greatest. *Judgment.* This is the single most actionable fact on this entire map, and its implication runs opposite to the way most people job-hunt. A man with nothing has no credentials to offer, which means he is in precisely the segment where the referral premium is largest and the credential deficit matters least.

***Analogy.*** Ground in the canon is unevenly passable; so is the labour market. Sun Tzu's distinction between ground you can traverse and ground where the army gets stuck matches the distinction between an arena entered by referral and one entered by open application. ***Breaks.*** Military ground is fixed for a campaign's duration. The labour market is made of decisions by people who can be influenced; its passability is partly endogenous to the man crossing it. No resolve makes a marsh firm; building a relationship does make a hiring manager receptive. Treat the labour market as ground for planning and as a population of actors for execution, and never confuse the two.

**Housing.** *Fact.* As of 2024, 22.7 million renter households — 49 percent of all renters — spent more than 30 percent of income on housing, and 12.1 million (26 percent) more than half, a rise of 2.3 million cost-burdened renters since 2019. Eleven million extremely low-income renter households compete for 3.8 million affordable and available units, and units renting under $1,000 a month in real terms fell by more than 7 million between 2014 and 2024. Home prices rose 54 percent nationally since 2020; in 2025 the median existing single-family sale price was nearly five times median household income; existing home sales sit at a three-decade low of 4.1 million.

*Mechanism.* Housing cost is a fixed tax on time. A man paying half his income for shelter cannot accumulate the reserve that lets him refuse a bad offer, retrain, or move. This is the modern form of the supply constraint dominating Sun Tzu's second chapter: a force whose consumption is fixed and high cannot campaign long, and must accept a worse decision sooner. ***Analogy.*** A tight fit — the logic of protraction ruining the victor translates almost directly. ***Breaks.*** An army's supply problem ends when the campaign ends. Rent does not. The modern man is permanently in the condition Sun Tzu treats as an emergency, which makes the doctrine built for emergencies — speed, decisiveness, take supply from the contested ground — his standing condition rather than an exception.

**Local versus remote, and cities as terrain.** *Fact.* Over the past year, 12 percent of US full-time employees were fully remote, 62 percent fully on site, 26 percent hybrid. Remote work is a real but minority arrangement; most work remains attached to a place. *Fact.* Residential mobility hit a record low of 11.2 percent in 2024, driven largely by homeowners locked in by below-market mortgage rates, with interstate moves also falling.

*Judgment.* Those two facts define the modern positional problem. Most work is still local, and almost nobody moves. For a man with nothing, the second is an opportunity rather than a constraint: he is one of the few actors free to move, having no mortgage, no equity, no locked-in rate and nothing to pack. Mobility is the one form of capital he begins with, and the form everyone around him has lost.

**The significance of place.** *Fact.* Studying more than seven million families who moved across US commuting zones and counties, Chetty and Hendren found that children who move to a better area — better as measured by the outcomes of children already living there — improve in proportion to the time they spend growing up there, at a rate of approximately 4 percent per year of exposure. The companion paper estimates the causal effect of each US county. ***Analogy.*** The closest thing in modern social science to Vegetius on the higher position: the ground itself confers advantage independent of the force standing on it, and the advantage accrues by occupation over time. ***Breaks.*** Two ways, both important. The exposure effect is measured on children and the mechanism is developmental; a 25-year-old who moves to a high-opportunity county does not get 4 percent a year. What the research establishes for him is weaker — that places differ causally, not that he personally captures the difference at that rate. And it measures counties, far larger than neighbourhoods, so it establishes that place matters at a coarse grain without telling him which street. *Judgment.* The honest translation: place is real terrain, choosing a metropolitan area is a strategic decision with measured consequences, and anyone quoting the 4 percent figure at a working-age adult is misusing it.

## 2. Lines of communication and supply

The canon's most consistent obsession, from Sun Tzu's cartload of enemy grain to Vauban's siege parallels, is that force is limited by what reaches it. Three modern lines carry the equivalent load: money, information, and physical movement.

**Money.** *Fact.* In 2023, 4.2 percent of US households — about 5.6 million — were unbanked, nobody in the household holding a checking or savings account. A further 14.2 percent, about 19.0 million households, were underbanked. The most-cited main reason for having no account was not meeting minimum balance requirements (23.3 percent), with distrust of banks second (15.7 percent); a third (33.4 percent) cited fees or minimum balances. Rates are sharply uneven: 11.2 percent for working-age households with a disability against 3.7 percent without, and 12.3 percent for single-parent households against 2.3 percent for married couples with children.

*Fact.* The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau reported about 26 million American adults are "credit invisible" — no credit history with a nationwide reporting agency, roughly one in ten adults — with a further 19 million holding files too thin or stale to score. Black consumers, Hispanic consumers and consumers in low-income neighbourhoods are likelier to have no history or insufficient history.

*Mechanism.* Banking and credit form one interlocking gate. Formal payroll is overwhelmingly electronic, so no account means either fee-bearing conversion of every paycheque or exclusion from formal employment. No credit file means no lease on normal terms, no car loan, higher utility deposits. The condition is self-reinforcing: the record that would end it is generated only by transactions that require it to exist. ***Analogy.*** This is a supply line in the strict sense — the channel through which resource reaches the force, narrow, and cutting it defeats an otherwise intact force without a battle. ***Breaks.*** Supply lines in war are attacked by an enemy who intends the harm. The credit gate is indifferent; nobody is trying to starve him. This matters because the canon's counter-doctrine — guard them, shorten them, or seize the enemy's — assumes an opponent whose intention can be anticipated. The correct modern counter is procedural, not tactical: satisfy the system's stated conditions in the order it recognises.

**Information flows.** *Fact.* The internet has made the formal descriptive knowledge of almost every trade and profession free at the point of use. Company filings sit at SEC EDGAR, wage distributions by occupation and metropolitan area are published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, and federal court records are on PACER at ten cents a page, capped at three dollars a document, with fees waived below thirty dollars a quarter. *Judgment.* The consequence is a reversal: information is no longer the scarce good. Attention, credibility, and access to the person who decides are. A man who believes his problem is that he does not know enough will spend years reading and remain where he started.

***Analogy.*** Intelligence in the canon is expensive, dangerous to obtain, and decisive — Sun Tzu's chapter on spies exists because knowing the enemy's dispositions cost lives and money. ***Breaks.*** This is the largest single break on the map. The modern man's problem is the inverse of the classical general's: he is not short of information but drowning in it, and his scarce resource is the judgment to discard most of it. Any chapter importing the canon's reverence for intelligence-gathering without inverting it for abundance sends the reader in exactly the wrong direction.

**Physical mobility.** *Fact.* A body of research on welfare-to-work transitions finds that car access is associated with better employment outcomes — employment, retention, earnings, hours — and that the relevant constraint is not merely distance but mode of transport, since a car permits jobs at hours and locations transit does not reach. I flag this literature as read in summary rather than in the primary papers; the direction of the finding is well replicated, the magnitudes I have seen quoted I could not verify.

*Mechanism.* A car, a licence and the insurance to run them are jointly a threshold good: below it, whole regions of the job market are invisible, because shifts starting before transit runs or ending after it stops cannot be accepted at all. ***Analogy.*** The canon's road network; Maurice and Vegetius on march rates and the passability of routes map cleanly onto it. ***Breaks.*** An army's mobility is an attribute of the whole force and is commanded. The modern man's is an individually purchased asset with a recurring cost — insurance, maintenance, fuel, the risk of a repair he cannot pay for — and that cost can itself ruin him. The asset that opens the terrain is also a liability that can sink him, which has no clean military parallel.

## 3. Fortresses and open field

Vauban's subject is the reduction of places that cannot be stormed. His answer — the approach by parallels, a systematic, un-heroic, scheduled advance under cover toward a point of entry — is the canon's most transferable single idea, and this section is where it applies.

**What the fortresses are.** *Fact.* Institutions that gate entry to remunerative work include licensing bodies, professional associations, universities, and employers' own credential filters. On licensing: in 2025 annual averages, 24.0 percent of employed US workers held a certification or license — 21.6 percent a license, 2.5 percent a certification only — meaning roughly three-quarters of employed people hold neither. *Reported, not verified in the primary document:* the Institute for Justice's third *License to Work* edition is said to catalogue 2,749 licenses across 102 lower-income occupations in the fifty states and DC, with average burdens of some 362 days of education and experience, at least one exam, and about $295 in fees.

*Judgment.* Read the verified number carefully; it cuts against the usual framing. Licensing is a real wall around specific occupations and falls hardest on lower-income trades, exactly where a man with nothing would otherwise enter. But three-quarters of employed Americans work without a license or certification of any kind. This is not primarily a licensed economy. The wall is high where it exists, and there is a great deal of open field beside it.

**Credential walls, and their softening.** *Fact.* Among states that removed degree requirements for public-sector roles, the share of postings explicitly requiring a bachelor's degree fell from 51.1 percent in the twelve months before the action to 41.8 percent in the twelve months after, with more than twenty states having removed or reassessed requirements over the preceding two years. *Fact and caution in the same source:* the researchers state plainly that it is too early to measure the effect on hiring behaviour. *Judgment.* This is a change in the stated rule, not yet a demonstrated change in who gets hired. A man planning around it is planning around a posting policy, and should treat the degree wall as lowered on paper and unverified in practice.

***Analogy.*** A credential is a curtain wall in the precise Vauban sense: not there to kill the attacker but to make unsystematic attack fail, so that only a besieger willing to proceed methodically gets in. The approach by parallels — a sequence of covered positions, each making the next possible, none of them a charge — structurally matches the sequence by which a man without credentials reaches gated work: an adjacent entry-level role, then an internal move, then a funded qualification, then the gated position. ***Breaks.*** Vauban besieges a place that cannot move and whose garrison must eventually capitulate to arithmetic. Institutions change their rules mid-siege, and none is obliged to capitulate to anyone in particular. The analogy is sound about method and false about inevitability.

**Gated versus ungated arenas.** ***Analogy.*** The governing distinction is Sun Tzu's ranking of targets, in which besieging walled cities is worst because it is most expensive per unit of result. The modern open field is any arena where output is directly observable and credentials are not checked before the output is looked at: the trades, sales, small-business work, freight and logistics, food service, construction, and the parts of software and creative work where a portfolio can be inspected. The modern walled city is medicine, law, accountancy, academia, the regulated professions, and most large-employer entry paths where an automated filter reads the application before a human does. ***Breaks.*** Sun Tzu ranks the walled city last because it can be bypassed; the campaign's object lies beyond it. Here the gated profession often *is* the object, and there is no route around it — only the slower route through.

*Fact.* Some low-gated routes have formal structures attached. Registered apprenticeship is a federally recognised route in which work is paid from the start and the credential is earned inside the job rather than purchased before it. *Judgment.* The strategic content of this section is one sentence: enter where output is visible before credentials are checked, and treat walled arenas as objectives approached from inside the economy rather than assaulted from outside it. That is Sun Tzu's target hierarchy and Vauban's method agreeing with each other, which is rare enough to trust.

## 4. Intelligence

**What is free.** *Fact.* Public company filings are free at SEC EDGAR, with full-text search across more than twenty years of filings. Occupational wage estimates by area are published free by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Federal court records are on PACER at ten cents a page, three dollars maximum per document, with quarterly fees under thirty dollars waived. State licensing requirements and disciplinary records are published by the licensing boards themselves. *Fact.* Pay is increasingly disclosed by law: as of 2026 a substantial and growing number of states and the District of Columbia require employers to state salary ranges in postings or on request — *reported from secondary compilations, which I could not verify against the statutes themselves.*

**What is private.** What an employer will actually pay this particular candidate; what the internal politics of the hiring decision are; who else is being considered; whether the posting corresponds to a real open position at all; what the manager's own position is. *Judgment.* The asymmetry is not between public and secret information — nearly all structural information is public. It is between structural information, which is free, and situational information, which is held by individuals and is obtainable only through relationships. This is why the referral finding in §1 and the intelligence problem here are the same problem seen twice.

**How asymmetry is exploited against him.** *Fact.* Employers and landlords buy consumer reports about applicants; applicants cannot see the report before the decision. The CFPB, analysing more than 24,000 complaints about tenant screening, found over 16,000 concerning inaccurate information and about 4,500 concerning difficulty correcting errors, noted that prior rental payment history appears in only 1.7 to 2.3 percent of US renters' reports, and found that roughly 68 percent of renters pay application fees that fund reports they generally cannot see before the decision is made.

*Mechanism.* He pays for the intelligence used against him, does not receive it, and is denied on a record whose accuracy he has no opportunity to contest in time. ***Analogy.*** The canon's asymmetric-knowledge doctrine — Sun Tzu's insistence that the enemy acts on his picture of you, and that the picture is therefore the controllable weapon. ***Breaks.*** In Sun Tzu the picture is controllable by the one pictured, through deception and concealment. Here it is not: it is assembled by third parties from records he did not create and cannot edit, and the legal remedies operate after the decision that mattered. The doctrine survives in reduced form — he cannot control the picture, but he can know what is in it before they do, by pulling his own reports first.

## 5. Weather and season

The canon treats weather as the factor that is real, consequential, and not subject to will — Sun Tzu's "heaven," which the general must read and cannot command.

**Verified conditions, autumn 2026.** *Fact.* In August 2026, nonfarm payrolls rose 162,000, the unemployment rate was unchanged at 4.1 percent, there were about 7.0 million unemployed, 1.9 million of them — 27.0 percent — jobless for 27 weeks or more, labour force participation was 61.6 percent, and average hourly earnings were $37.75, up 3.1 percent over twelve months. *Fact.* In August 2026 the consumer price index rose 0.4 percent on the month and 3.4 percent over twelve months; core inflation ran 2.4 percent; the energy index was up 16.3 percent over the year with gasoline up 27.4 percent; shelter was up 3.0 percent. *Fact.* On 16 September 2026 the Federal Open Market Committee raised the federal funds target range by a quarter point to 3¾ to 4 percent on a 12–0 vote, stating that inflation remains elevated, that job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and that economic activity is expanding at a solid pace.

*Judgment.* This is a specific and unusual weather system, and the man entering now should understand it rather than the generic recession he may be braced for. Employment is stable but slack is accumulating in duration — over a quarter of the unemployed have been out more than six months, the number that matters for anyone who may soon join them. Wage growth at 3.1 percent against headline inflation at 3.4 percent means real pay is flat to slightly negative. The inflation sits in energy, not the broad core, which is why the central bank is tightening into a stable labour market rather than loosening into a weak one. Rates are rising. Any plan assuming cheap borrowing is a plan against the current weather.

**Timing within the year.** *Fact.* Hiring has a genuine seasonal structure; this is not in doubt, because the federal statistical agencies seasonally adjust the hiring and openings series precisely to remove a known and measured within-year pattern. *Unverified:* the specific magnitudes widely quoted — that January sees 10 to 20 percent more postings than a typical month, and that Q1 and September–October are peaks — come from recruiting-industry sources I could not trace to primary data, and I do not rely on them.

***Analogy.*** Weather in the canon is the archetypal factor that must be waited for rather than forced, and Sun Tzu's coupling of heaven with earth as the two givens fits the macroeconomy plus the hiring calendar. ***Breaks.*** Military weather is short-cycle and local; a commander waits out a storm in days. The business cycle runs in years, longer than a man with no savings can wait, so the classical counsel of patience is unaffordable at individual scale. *Judgment.* The correct translation is not "wait for good weather" but "know which weather you are in, and choose the action robust across the ones you might be in." A campaign that only works if rates fall is staked on a forecast.

## 6. The other actors

The canon knows one enemy and one sovereign. The modern field holds many actors with divergent interests, most of whom are not adversaries at all. Naming what each wants, fears, and can see is the section that most repays precision, because most bad life-strategy advice comes from treating every counterparty as an opponent.

**Employers.** *Want:* output at predictable cost, and above all not to make a hiring mistake, which is expensive in money, time and managerial attention. *Fear:* the bad hire, the fast quit, legal exposure. *Can see:* the application, the credential, the consumer report, the referral, whatever a search returns. *Cannot see:* whether he will actually be good — the thing they most need to know. *Mechanism.* Every feature of modern hiring — filters, degree requirements, background checks, referral bonuses — is a cheap proxy for an expensive unobservable. *Judgment.* This reframes the encounter. The employer is not an enemy to be defeated but a party under uncertainty to be reassured, and every proxy is an invitation to supply better evidence on the same axis.

**Landlords.** *Want:* rent on time, no damage, no eviction proceeding. *Fear:* a non-paying tenant, because removal is slow and costly. *Can see:* credit file, eviction records, income multiple, references. *Cannot see:* reliability. *Fact.* The eviction record is the dominant screening signal and the records are unreliable — the CFPB documented over-inclusion through automated matching, records belonging to other people, and obsolete entries past the seven-year limit. *Fact, reported only:* the Eviction Lab puts average annual filings at about 3.6 million between 2000 and 2018 against roughly 2.7 million unique households, the gap reflecting repeat filings.

**Lenders.** *Want:* repayment with interest, priced to risk. *Fear:* default and regulatory penalty. *Can see:* the credit file, nothing else. *Cannot see:* a man with no file, who is not a bad risk but an unmeasured one. *Judgment.* The credit-invisible man is not rejected for being poor but for being unreadable, which is a different and more tractable problem.

**The state.** *Wants:* tax compliance, and — through benefit programmes — to support income without destroying the incentive to earn. *Fears:* fraud and cost. *Can see:* reported income, filings, enrolments. *Fact.* The federal minimum wage has been $7.25 an hour since 24 July 2009; many states set higher floors, and where both apply the employer must comply with both. *Fact, in outline and widely documented in the policy literature:* benefits phase out as earnings rise, and when several programmes phase out over the same income range their withdrawal rates stack, producing effective marginal tax rates on additional earnings that can be very high. I read this in summary, not in the primary programme rules; the stacking mechanism is not in dispute, the specific rates vary by state and household, and I cite none.

*Mechanism.* The benefit cliff is a terrain feature the canon has no name for: a region where advancing costs more than standing still. ***Analogy.*** The nearest match is Sun Tzu's "hemmed-in ground," where the route forward is narrow and the force is punished for entering. ***Breaks.*** Hemmed-in ground is escaped by stratagem or by not entering. The benefit cliff is escaped only by crossing it fast enough that the losing region is traversed in one move rather than inhabited — a jump in earnings, not an increment. *Judgment.* This is the strongest argument in this file against incrementalism for a man on assistance, and it is an argument from arithmetic, not character.

**Platforms.** *Want:* transaction volume and engagement. *Fear:* liability, and users leaving. *Can see:* behaviour in enormous detail. *Cannot see:* him. *Fact.* Twenty percent of US adults performed some gig activity in the prior month; the most common was selling items (13 percent), then short-term tasks (9 percent), then platform-arranged work such as delivery or rideshare (4 percent). Seventy percent of gig workers spent under five hours a week on it; 31 percent said it was essential to making ends meet; 65 percent of gig workers reported doing okay or living comfortably against 75 percent of non-gig workers. *Judgment.* Platform work is a genuine floor — income within days, no credential, no referral — and a poor ceiling. Treat it as foraging in Sun Tzu's sense: it sustains the force in motion and is not a position to hold.

**Competitors.** Other applicants for the same entry-level positions. *Want:* the same job. *Can see:* the same posting. ***Analogy.*** The only actor here occupying the canon's "enemy" slot, and even so the fit is poor: competitors do not act against him, they occupy the same space. ***Breaks.*** War is zero-sum over a contested object and the enemy adapts to you. Job competition is zero-sum only per position, in a market with millions of monthly hires, against rivals who do not know he exists. *Judgment.* Treating other applicants as an enemy is the most common and most wasteful error in this domain. The binding constraint is almost never that someone beat him; it is that no decision-maker ever saw him.

**Allies and mentors.** *Want:* variously — competence around them, reciprocity, status, the pleasure of being consulted. *Fear:* wasting effort on someone who will not follow through, and reputational cost from vouching badly. *Mechanism.* Given §1, the referral is the highest-value transferable asset in this economy, and its price is the voucher's reputation. *Judgment.* That price explains everything about how mentorship actually works and why asking for a referral cold almost never succeeds: he is asking a stranger to stake reputation on an unobservable. Demonstrated reliability over time is not politeness, it is the only currency that buys the asset.

## 7. The element with no military analogue

Three modern structures have no counterpart in the canon. Where the canon is silent, this file does not invent a parallel.

**Algorithmic gatekeeping.** *Fact.* Automated systems now filter applications before a human reads them. The Harvard Business School and Accenture study *Hidden Workers: Untapped Talent*, published 3 September 2021 by Fuller, Raman, Sage-Gavin and Hines, documented that standard hiring processes systematically exclude qualified workers including caregivers, veterans, the formerly incarcerated and people with disabilities, and that firms recruiting from these groups report better loyalty and performance than from traditional sources. *Reported from secondary coverage, not the report PDF itself:* the widely quoted figures of 27 million "hidden workers" and 88 percent of employers agreeing that qualified candidates are screened out by exact-criteria filters. *Fact.* The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission's first settlement over algorithmic hiring discrimination came on 11 September 2023, when iTutorGroup paid $365,000 over allegations under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act that it had programmed its application software to automatically reject female applicants aged 55 or older and male applicants aged 60 or older, affecting more than 200 qualified US applicants.

*Mechanism.* A filter encoding a proxy applies it at scale, instantly, without review, and without the applicant learning it ran. *Judgment.* The iTutorGroup case matters less for the money than for how the discrimination was detected: an applicant submitted two otherwise identical applications differing only in date of birth. The filter was invisible until someone probed it. That is the defining property of this terrain feature — undetectable from outside by ordinary means.

**Regulation of it is unsettled.** *Reported, not verified against the statutes:* New York City's Local Law 144 requires annual independent bias audits of automated employment decision tools, public disclosure of results, and candidate notice; Colorado's SB 24-205 was delayed and then, in 2026, repealed and replaced with a different disclosure framework. *Judgment.* The pattern is a live and unstable regulatory area, and any book chapter that states the law here will date within a year. Describe the mechanism; do not state the rule.

**The attention and reputation economy.** *Fact, with a weak source, flagged accordingly.* Employer screening of candidates' public online presence is widely reported at around 70 percent, from a 2018 CareerBuilder survey repeated endlessly since; I could not verify a current figure and treat the practice as common rather than quantified. *Fact.* Search results, ratings and public posts are durable, indexed, and in the United States carry no general right to erasure of lawfully published material.

*Mechanism.* Reputation was once local, decaying and escapable by moving. It is now global, permanent and portable. ***Why the analogy fails.*** The canon's treatment of reputation — Machiavelli on being feared or loved, Sun Tzu on the appearance one projects — assumes reputation is a controlled projection by the actor toward an audience. The modern version is an indexed record assembled partly by others, persisting beyond the actor's control and beyond the relevance of its contents. No classical actor's youthful error remained keyword-searchable for thirty years. *Judgment.* The consequence is asymmetric: the digital record is cheap to damage and expensive to repair, which makes this a domain where defensive discipline dominates offensive effort. The corollary is the rare genuine opportunity — because the record is permanent and searchable, a small body of visible, verifiable work compounds in a way word-of-mouth never did.

## 8. Law as terrain

Descriptive only. What follows describes the ground; it does not advise crossing it anywhere in particular, and none of it is legal advice.

**The criminal record.** *Fact.* The American Bar Association's National Inventory of the Collateral Consequences of Conviction catalogues legal restrictions attaching to conviction across all fifty states, DC, the territories and the federal government — *the widely cited total of more than 44,000 separate consequences I could not confirm on the inventory's own pages, and mark as reported.* *Fact.* Criminal background checks are near-universal in US hiring, reported at roughly 93 to 96 percent of employers — *industry-survey sourced, not verified.* *Reported:* Devah Pager's matched-pair audit study in Milwaukee found applicants disclosing a non-violent drug conviction received callbacks at between a half and a third the rate of otherwise identical applicants, and that Black applicants without a record fared worse than white applicants with one. That study founds the field; I report it from secondary accounts.

*Fact.* Relief exists and is expanding: automatic record-clearing statutes, commonly called clean slate laws, have been enacted in a number of states with staggered implementation through 2026 and 2027 — *counts and dates from compliance-vendor compilations, unverified.*

*Mechanism.* A conviction is not one barrier but a bundle — employment screening, licensing bars, housing screening, sometimes benefit eligibility — each operating independently, so clearing one leaves the others standing. ***Analogy.*** Vauban's defence in depth: successive independent works, each reduced separately, the reduction of one conferring no entry past the next. ***Breaks.*** A fortress's works are held by one garrison with one commander who can surrender them all. These are held by different authorities who do not coordinate and cannot surrender on each other's behalf.

**Legal but ruinous.** *Fact.* Payday lending is legal in much of the country and structurally punishing: the CFPB's research describes a median storefront fee of about $15 per $100 borrowed on a median fourteen-day term, which is an annual percentage rate near 390 to 400 percent — *cited from the agency's published research as reported in secondary summaries; I did not open the primary CFPB data point.* *Fact.* Non-compete agreements remain enforceable under state law: the Federal Trade Commission's rule banning most of them was blocked by a district court on 20 August 2024, the Commission moved to dismiss its appeal on 5 September 2025, and the FTC's own page now states that "The Noncompete Rule is not in effect and it is not enforceable." *Judgment.* Other items in this category — signing away claims in an arbitration clause, a car loan at a rate exceeding the asset's depreciation, a job classified as independent contracting without understanding the tax consequence — share one property: entered voluntarily, fully legal, with costs arriving long after the decision. That delay is the mechanism. It is not ignorance of the law that ruins people here but the lag between signature and consequence.

**Illegal but common.** *Fact, in outline.* Unpaid overtime and off-the-clock work, misclassification of employees as contractors, cash payment without withholding, and rental practices violating housing law are documented and routine. I state the category without quantifying it: the credible enforcement statistics I found measure recovered wages rather than prevalence, and I will not convert one into the other. *Judgment.* The strategically relevant feature is asymmetry of consequence. When an employer breaks these rules the sanction is usually financial, late, and borne by an entity. When the worker breaks the corresponding rules — undeclared income, most obviously — the sanction attaches to him personally and follows him. The canon's assumption that both sides face the same law of war does not hold here.

## 9. Where the analogy holds, and where it must be abandoned

**Holds.** Supply constraining action (housing cost, §1). Position conferring advantage independent of the force occupying it (place effects, §1). Methodical approach to a work that cannot be stormed (credential walls, §3). Asymmetric knowledge as decisive (§4). Weather as real, consequential, uncommandable (§5). Defence in depth as successive independent barriers (§8).

**Breaks, and in what direction.** Intelligence is abundant, not scarce, which inverts the canon's posture toward information (§2, §4). There is no enemy: the actors that matter are indifferent or willing, and treating them as opponents misallocates the whole effort (§6). The terrain responds to relationship in a way ground never does (§1). Timescales do not match — weather is waited out, the business cycle cannot be (§5). And the permanent digital record has no classical counterpart at all (§7).

**Judgment, as this file's conclusion.** The canon transfers best where it is about economy — cost, duration, supply, the ranking of objectives by expense — and worst where it is about enmity. Sun Tzu's second chapter and Vauban's method survive translation almost intact. The doctrine of deceiving an opponent does not, because there is no opponent. A book that takes the economic canon seriously and drops the adversarial canon will be more useful and more honest than one that keeps the swords.
